## Description

Anesthetic and analgesic used in emergency and veterinary medicine. Produces dissociative states — feelings of mind-body separation. White crystalline powder. Routes: nasal (most common recreational), oral, injection. Psychological dependence can develop.

## Effects

**Low doses:** Mildly euphoric, alcohol-like.

**Higher doses:** Progressive separation of psyche from body; floating sensation; communication becomes difficult; emotions feel flat; pain disappears; movement severely restricted.

**Hallucinations:** "Cold, dark visions," tunnel imagery. High doses: near-death experiences (psychologically distressing).

**Physical:** Nystagmus, nausea/vomiting, ataxia, elevated pulse/blood pressure, life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias. Very high doses: muscle rigidity, rhabdomyolysis, paralysis, seizures, coma.

## Duration

| Route | Onset | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Oral | ~15–20 min | 30 min – 3 hours |
| Nasal | ~5 min | 30 min – 3 hours |
| Intramuscular | 2–5 min | 30 min – 3 hours |

## Dosage

Two forms circulate: **S-Ketamine** (~2× potency of racemic). Reliable dosage figures cannot be given — start with smallest possible amount.

## Risks

### Long-Term
- Psychological dependence
- Impairment of memory, learning, perception
- **Urological damage**: bloody urine, painful urination, incontinence — potentially irreversible, may require surgical intervention

### Dangerous Combinations
- **+ Depressants (alcohol, benzos, opioids)**: Risk of respiratory paralysis increases drastically

## Contraindications
- Cardiovascular disease, high blood pressure, tachycardia
- Low mood, agitation, anxiety, psychiatric conditions
- Pregnancy/breastfeeding

## Safer Use

1. If feeling unsure, do not consume
2. **Never use alone** — arrange someone to watch over you
3. Effects less overwhelming in low-stimulus environments
4. Avoid all polydrug use, especially with depressants
5. If someone loses consciousness or has breathing difficulties → recovery position → call emergency services
