## PsychonautWiki — Poppers — https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Poppers — accessed 2026-07-24

Poppers is the common name for **alkyl nitrites**: amyl nitrite, isobutyl nitrite,
isopropyl nitrite, butyl nitrite and others; amyl nitrite is the most widely sold.
They are volatile liquids sold as "video head cleaners", "polish removers" or "room
deodorisers" whose fumes are inhaled, particularly before or during sex. The usual
method is to open the bottle, hold it under one nostril and inhale. **Effects start
after about 15 seconds and last up to about 3 minutes.** The mechanism is reduction
to nitric oxide, a gasotransmitter that relaxes smooth muscle, producing vasodilation
("blood rushing to the brain"), muscle relaxation, increased heart rate, decreased
blood pressure, headaches, euphoria, tactile enhancement, disinhibition and increased
libido.

Safety warnings are extensive. **Interaction:** "Alkyl nitrites are interactive with
other vasodilators like sildenafil (Viagra), vardenafil (Levitra), tadalafil (Cialis)
and nitroglycerin to cause a serious decrease in blood pressure, which can lead to
fainting." **Oral use:** "other methods of popper usage such as oral use are extremely
dangerous and can potentially result in death or coma" — ingestion can cause cyanosis,
unconsciousness, coma and death via methaemoglobinaemia (treatable with methylene blue).
**Vision:** alkyl nitrites are a possible, rare cause of maculopathy (eye damage), with
documented foveal damage in habitual users; vision loss reports increased after the
2006 shift to isopropyl nitrite. **Physical hazards:** they burn skin on contact and are
extremely flammable — avoid use near smoking or candles. Nitrites substantially decrease
blood pressure (fainting risk); accidental aspiration can cause lipoid pneumonia. Little
addictive potential and no meaningful tolerance are noted.

## TripSit — Use of Poppers — https://updates.tripsit.me/use-of-poppers/ — accessed 2026-07-24

TripSit describes poppers as a group of inhalant drugs, most commonly amyl nitrite,
with other alkyl nitrites sometimes substituted. **Safest use:** "placing the bottle of
the substance near the nose and inhaling for a maximum of five seconds." Within seconds
users feel a rushing sensation, warmth and euphoria "which will last a maximum of 3–5
minutes, with the primary effects passing within one minute." The effects come from
blood pressure being lowered quickly, "resulting in blood rushing to the heart and
brain, causing light-headedness and an increase in heart rate."

**Critical dangers.** Ingestion: "Alkyl Nitrites are indeed potentially deadly when
ingested, and this should always be avoided. If you drink poppers, seek medical
attention immediately." Overdose: "With increased doses (generally, amount of time
inhaled) users may potentially become unconscious or enter a coma," though this is hard
to reach with the small bottles usually sold. Cardiovascular: "Poppers should also be
avoided by those with heart problems." Dependence/redosing: poppers "aren't believed to
be particularly addictive, though the short length of effect may cause users to redose
several times, which can cause headaches"; users should take breaks to avoid stressing
the cardiovascular system and compounding effects between doses. (This TripSit factsheet
does not itself discuss PDE5-inhibitor interactions or flammability — those are covered
by the other sources here.) Note: TripSit's machine-readable **combination chart does not
include poppers/nitrites** as a row — only "Nitrous" (nitrous oxide, a different drug).

## DanceSafe — Poppers — https://dancesafe.org/poppers/ — accessed 2026-07-24

DanceSafe defines poppers as a class of "alkyl nitrites" — amyl, butyl, isobutyl and
isopropyl nitrite — sold in little bottles as "video head cleaners", "polish removers"
or "room deodorizers", converting to gas immediately when opened. **Effects "are felt
within a few seconds and last for one to four minutes, sometimes a little longer,"**
working by relaxing smooth muscle around blood vessels, dilating them and increasing
heart rate; poppers also relax smooth muscle in the vagina and anus, easing receptive
sex. Common effects include euphoria, feeling "in the moment", dizziness/room-spinning,
and headaches from cerebral vasodilation.

**Administration:** "Poppers are usually inhaled by holding one nostril and gently
breathing in air that's wafted from the bottle." Always waft the air toward your nose —
never place the bottle directly against your nostrils, as the liquid on your skin causes
burns. **Safety:** "Swallowing the liquid in the bottle is extremely dangerous and can
be fatal." "Combining poppers and Viagra can lead to a massive drop in blood pressure,
which is very dangerous." Poppers pose "extra risks for pregnant people and people who
have heart problems (like arrhythmias), abnormal blood pressure, a history of cerebral
hemorrhaging, or anemia." Mixing with stimulants raises the risk of overheating and
heart/blood-pressure problems. "Poppers are highly flammable. Keep away from cigarettes,
candles, and lighters." Isobutyl nitrite was found by the EU to be possibly carcinogenic.
Because poppers are unregulated, exact contents are unknown and untested.

## Talk to FRANK — Alkyl nitrites (Poppers) — https://talktofrank.com/drug/poppers — accessed 2026-07-24

FRANK (UK government drug information) describes poppers as a liquid chemical (alkyl
nitrites) sold in small bottles under names like Liquid Gold and Bang Aroma; they dilate
blood vessels to allow more blood to reach the heart. Effects are "a head-rush that lasts
for a couple of minutes", a sense of euphoria, and increased sex drive and skin
sensitivity; negatives include feeling sick, faint or uncoordinated, nosebleeds,
headaches and chest pains. "Although the high from poppers is over in a few minutes, you
may feel dizzy or sick after taking them — and you may get a headache."

**Risks.** "Swallowing poppers can be fatal." "Fatal 'sudden sniffing death syndrome' has
been reported due to development of an abnormal heart rhythm." "Sniffing poppers can make
your blood pressure drop." Crucially: "You shouldn't take them if you have problems with
your blood pressure, are on any blood pressure medication, or if you're taking erectile
dysfunction medication such as Viagra." Anaemia and glaucoma patients should avoid use.
Vision: "there have been reports of people experiencing temporary and permanent loss of
vision after using poppers" and "repeated use can damage your eyesight" (poppers
maculopathy, linked to isopropyl nitrite). Chemical burns can cause rashes around the
nose and mouth. Mixing with alcohol increases the risk of unconsciousness and choking.
Sexual disinhibition may lead to riskier practices and has been linked to STI
transmission and injury during sex.

## Release — Alkyl Nitrites (Poppers): Harm Reduction — https://www.release.org.uk/drugs/alkyl-nitrites-poppers/harm-reduction — accessed 2026-07-24

Release (UK drugs law/harm-reduction charity) gives practical safer-use guidance.
**Never swallow:** "Poppers are a toxic chemical substance and should not be swallowed."
Overdoses are extremely rare and essentially occur only when the liquid is mistakenly
ingested rather than inhaled. **Blood pressure / contraindications:** people with low
blood pressure should be "particularly wary about using poppers"; use is also inadvisable
for anyone with chest problems, heart conditions, anaemia or glaucoma. **Skin & fire:**
"They burn the skin so any contact with the skin should be avoided"; splashing can cause
rashes, sore throats and eye irritation; the substance is "highly flammable, so should
not [come into] contact with naked flames."

**Common side effects** are reversible on stopping: headaches (most prevalent, and worse
with stale product), vomiting, dizziness, sweating, fainting and coughing.
**Methaemoglobin risk:** poppers can convert haemoglobin to methaemoglobin; significant
conversion causes "breathlessness, headaches and the tongue and lips to turn blue."
Continuous use is inadvisable — users should take breaks, and stop if these symptoms
appear. (This page does not itself detail the PDE5-inhibitor interaction; see FRANK,
DanceSafe and PsychonautWiki for that warning.) The blue-lips/breathlessness signs are a
useful field cue that too much has been absorbed and oxygen transport is compromised.

## Corkery, Schifano et al. 2025 — "An Update on Deaths in the United Kingdom from 'Poppers' (Alkyl Nitrites), with a Particular Focus on 'Swallowing' Fatalities" — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11765549/ — accessed 2026-07-24

A peer-reviewed UK mortality review. **At least 42 poppers-related deaths occurred during
1987–2018**; the review focuses on the subset caused by **swallowing** rather than
inhaling. Mode of use was known in 23/42 cases; the product was "definitely swallowed in
five cases, and very likely in a further one," and the authors identify **10 confirmed or
probable swallowing deaths** in total. Scenarios included accidental ingestion (mistaking
bottles for methadone or energy drinks), deliberate consumption while intoxicated, and
children accessing unsecured bottles.

**Mechanism when swallowed:** "If poppers are swallowed, cyanosis can occur due to the
conversion of haemoglobin to methaemoglobin, which compromises oxygen transport." Recorded
methaemoglobin levels in the case studies were 38% and 46%, with higher levels (70–95%)
noted in some overseas fatalities. **PDE5 interaction confirmed as a fatal factor:** Case
9 was a 42-year-old man found with "two pots of Viagra"; toxicology detected tadalafil
(Cialis) and the cause of death was recorded as "combined alkyl nitrate and tadalafil
toxicity." The authors also note that "the co-use of alcohol and/or psychoactive
substances can impair an individual's risk assessment concerning the use of 'poppers.'"
**Harm-reduction conclusions:** child-proof containers, clearer manufacturer/retailer
warnings about swallowing, and inclusion of poppers in school PSHE education. This is the
strongest clinical evidence that the two headline dangers — swallowing (methaemoglobinaemia)
and combining with erection drugs — are real, documented causes of death.
