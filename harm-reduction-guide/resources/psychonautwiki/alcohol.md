# Alcohol — PsychonautWiki

**Source:** https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Alcohol

## Description
Ethanol (also known as ethyl alcohol, drinking alcohol or simply alcohol) is a depressant substance of the alcohol class. It is the primary psychoactive component of alcoholic drinks; the second-most widely used recreational substance globally (after caffeine). Ethanol principally acts by binding to GABA receptors but also interacts with various neurotransmitters and receptor sites. Alcohol is classified as a Group 1 carcinogen by the WHO — "there is no safe amount that does not affect health."

## Subjective Effects
- Sedation
- Disinhibition
- Anxiety suppression
- Empathy, affection and sociability enhancement
- Muscle relaxation
- Euphoria

## Dosage (Oral — pure ethanol grams)
- Threshold: 10 g (~1 standard drink)
- Light: 10–20 g (1–2 standard drinks)
- Common: 20–30 g (2–3 standard drinks)
- Strong: 30–40 g (3–4 standard drinks)
- Heavy: 40+ g (4+ standard drinks)

## Duration (Oral)
- Total: 1.5–5 hours
- Onset: 15–30 minutes
- Come up: 15–30 minutes
- Peak: 30–90 minutes
- Offset: 45–120 minutes
- After effects: 6–48 hours

## Toxicity and Harm Potential
- Therapeutic index of ethanol is only 10:1.
- Long-term effects range from potential cardioprotective benefits (low-moderate use) to severe damage from chronic abuse.
- Associated with increased risk of alcoholism, malnutrition, chronic pancreatitis, liver disease, cancer.
- Damages central and peripheral nervous systems.
- Damaging to nearly every organ and system with long-term use.
- Developing adolescent brain is particularly vulnerable.
- Fetal alcohol syndrome (FAS) may result from maternal consumption.
- Classified as Group 1 carcinogen (IARC): causes breast, colorectal, larynx, liver, esophageal, oral cavity, pharynx cancer.
- Moderate abuse potential with chronic use associated with tolerance, physical dependence, and addiction.
- Fatal overdose may occur when combined with other depressants (opiates, benzodiazepines, barbiturates, gabapentinoids).

## Safer Use
- Start with lower amounts to gauge tolerance.
- Pace drinking and alternate with water.
- Eat before and during drinking to slow absorption.
- Never combine with other depressants.
- Use harm reduction practices.
