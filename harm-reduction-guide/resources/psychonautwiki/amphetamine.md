# Amphetamine — PsychonautWiki

**Source:** https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Amphetamine

## Description
Amphetamine (also known as alpha-methylphenethylamine, amfetamine, speed, Adderall, Dexedrine) is a classical stimulant substance of the phenethylamine class. It is the parent compound of the substituted amphetamines (methamphetamine, MDMA, cathinone, bupropion). The mechanism of action involves promoting release of dopamine and norepinephrine.

## Subjective Effects
- Stimulation
- Focus enhancement
- Motivation enhancement
- Increased libido
- Appetite suppression
- Euphoria
- Lower doses: increased focus and productivity
- Higher doses: increased sociability, sexual desire, euphoria

## Dosage (Oral)
- Threshold: 3 mg
- Light: 5–15 mg
- Common: 15–30 mg
- Strong: 30–50 mg
- Heavy: 50+ mg

## Dosage (Insufflated)
- Threshold: 2 mg
- Light: 3–10 mg
- Common: 10–25 mg
- Strong: 25–40 mg
- Heavy: 40+ mg

## Duration (Oral)
- Total: 6–10 hours
- Onset: 15–30 minutes
- Come up: 30–60 minutes
- Peak: 2.5–4 hours
- Offset: 2–3 hours
- After effects: 6–24 hours

## Toxicity and Harm Potential
- No evidence that amphetamine is directly neurotoxic in humans (as of 2014 research).
- High-dose amphetamine can cause indirect neurotoxicity via increased oxidative stress and dopamine autoxidation.
- Hyperpyrexia (core body temperature ≥40°C) necessary for development of amphetamine-induced neurotoxicity in animal models.
- Melatonin has been shown to prevent (pre-dose) and reverse amphetamine-induced neurotoxicity in rodent studies.
- High abuse potential; chronic use associated with compulsive redosing, escalating tolerance, and psychological dependence.
- Abuse linked to cardiovascular issues (high blood pressure, increased stroke risk).

## Safer Use
- Start with lower doses to assess individual response.
- Avoid frequent use — take breaks to prevent dependence.
- Monitor cardiovascular health (blood pressure, heart rate).
- Consider melatonin supplementation (take 30+ minutes before dosing).
- Stay hydrated and maintain nutrition despite appetite suppression.
- Use harm reduction practices.
