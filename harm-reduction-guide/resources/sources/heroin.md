## TripSit — Heroin — https://wiki.tripsit.me/wiki/Heroin — accessed 2026-08-31

TripSit's heroin factsheet carries a dosage table for **two routes only**.

**Intravenous:** common (no tolerance) 5–15 mg; strong (no tolerance) 10–20 mg;
common (with tolerance) 20–30 mg; strong (with tolerance) 30–50 mg.
**Smoked:** common 10–20 mg; strong 20–30 mg.

There is **no insufflated table**, and no threshold / light / heavy tiers for any route. The page's only
qualifier on the numbers is: **"Note: Purity varies wildly, and with such the dose does also."**

Two things this page does **not** say, both of which had been attributed to it in `protocols.json`:

1. It never states that its tiers are **street weight** rather than pure diamorphine. Its IV
   no-tolerance figures (5–15 mg common) read as active-substance doses. Asserting "street weight, not
   pure diamorphine" on TripSit's authority overstates the source (context.md rule 1.4).
2. It contains **no lethal dose, LD50, or toxicity threshold** of any kind. The "about 30 mg injected or
   about 60 mg by other routes" figure on the dosing card was cited to this page and is not on it. Both
   halves are real, but they come from elsewhere: the 30 mg is Drugchecking Berlin's and is specific to IV,
   the 60 mg is SaferParty's and carries no route at all. "By other routes" was the in-house extrapolation.

The page does carry harm-reduction warnings on overdose after a period of abstinence and on dangerous
combinations.

## SaferParty (Zürich) — Heroin — https://www.saferparty.ch/substanzen/heroin — accessed 2026-08-31

Swiss drug-checking service; German original.

**Lethal dose.** "Die tödliche Dosis liegt bei Personen ohne Toleranz bei ca. **60 mg reinem Heroin**."
A single figure, for pure heroin, in someone without tolerance. **No route split is given** — the page does
not say injecting is lethal at half the dose of other routes.

**Dosing.** "Die Dosis hängt stark vom Präparat bzw. dem Reinheitsgrad und von der individuellen Toleranz
ab." The page gives **no route-specific mg figures at all** (none for sniffen, rauchen or injizieren). It
therefore cannot support a tier table for any route.

**Pharmaceutical vs street.** Pharmaceutical diacetylmorphine (Diaphin, used in Swiss heroin-assisted
treatment) is dosed roughly **5× higher** than street heroin because of its purity. This supports the
guide's "several times stronger by weight than typical street heroin".

**Tolerance loss.** Overdose risk is particularly high "nach längeren Konsumunterbrüchen (kein Konsum von
mehr als **2 Tagen**)" — the source of the guide's 2-day threshold, which is unusually short and is stated
by SaferParty exactly that way.

## FDA-approved NARCAN (naloxone HCl) nasal spray label — DailyMed — https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=724df050-5332-4d0a-9a5f-17bf08a547e1&type=display — accessed 2026-08-31

The authoritative statement of what naloxone actually does, for the route a bystander will have.

**Onset / re-dosing.** "Seek emergency medical care immediately after use." "Administer additional doses of
NARCAN Nasal Spray, using a new nasal spray with each dose, if the patient does not respond or responds and
then relapses into respiratory depression, additional doses of NARCAN Nasal Spray may be given **every 2 to
3 minutes** until emergency medical assistance arrives."

The 2-to-3-minute re-dose interval is the label's own definition of how long you wait before concluding a
dose has not worked. It contradicts the guide's previous "reverses respiratory depression within seconds",
which risks a bystander deciding the naloxone failed and giving up.

**Recurrent depression.** "The duration of action of most opioids may exceed that of NARCAN Nasal Spray
resulting in a return of respiratory and/or central nervous system depression after an initial improvement
in symptoms." Confirms the guide's "naloxone wears off faster than heroin".

**Precipitated withdrawal.** "The use of NARCAN Nasal Spray in patients who are opioid-dependent may
precipitate opioid withdrawal characterized by the following signs and symptoms: body aches, diarrhea,
tachycardia, fever, runny nose, sneezing, piloerection, sweating, yawning, nausea or vomiting, nervousness,
restlessness or irritability, shivering or trembling, abdominal cramps, weakness, and increased blood
pressure."

**Harmless if it is not opioids.** "The medicine in NARCAN Nasal Spray has no effect in people who are not
taking opioid medicines." This is the basis for the "if unsure, give it anyway" default.

## American Heart Association — 2025 Guidelines, Part 7: Adult Basic Life Support — https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001369 — accessed 2026-08-31

Opioid overdose is the one overdose on this site where the arrest is **hypoxic**, not cardiac, and the AHA
treats it differently from the general compression-only default.

- **Lay rescuers (Class 1, LOE B-NR):** for an adult or child with suspected opioid overdose who is
  unresponsive and not breathing normally, **CPR with breaths** should be provided.
- **Trained rescuers (Class 1, LOE B-NR):** with a definite pulse and respiratory depression or arrest,
  provide rescue breaths or bag-mask ventilation.
- **Naloxone (Class 1, LOE B-NR):** should be given for respiratory arrest from suspected opioid overdose.
  Trained rescuers, lay rescuers and the general public can all administer it.

The AHA explicitly contrasts this with the compression-only advice usually given to untrained bystanders in
cardiac arrest. *(ahajournals.org returns 403 to automated fetches; the guideline text above was obtained
through site-restricted search of ahajournals.org and cpr.heart.org. The corresponding
"Opioid-Associated Emergency" algorithm PDFs are published at cpr.heart.org.)*

## BKA data via REITOX / DHS Workbook "Drogenmärkte und Kriminalität" 2024 — https://www.dhs.de/fileadmin/user_upload/REITOX_BERICHT_2024_WB_08_Drogenmaerkte_Kriminalitaet.pdf — accessed 2026-08-31

German seizure analysis, the source for any statement about German street purity. PDF fetched and text
extracted locally.

**Street level (untere Handelsebene), average purity:** 2019 **25.8 %** (peak), 2020 **24.6 %**,
2022 **20.3 %**, 2023 **13.5 %** — "damit bei den niedrigsten Werten seit 2014".
**Middle level (mittlere Handelsebene):** 2023 **16.9 %** (2022: 22.0 %).
**Wholesale (Großhandel), median:** 2020 **52.5 %**, 2021 **27.1 %**, 2022 **35.5 %**, 2023 **32.5 %**.

A 2021 street-level figure is **not** stated in this table. The guide's 18.7 % figure traces instead to
Drugchecking Berlin (local mirror `drugchecking-berlin/heroin.md`), which gives it as the 2021 German street
average with a 10-72 % range. It is sourced, but it is now two years out of date: the BKA street-level series
above has since fallen to 13.5 %.

**Adulterants:** of 1,517 samples analysed for cutting agents, caffeine and paracetamol each appeared in
more than 99 %.

**Synthetic opioids in German heroin, 2023:** of 1,595 samples, "Aus den vorliegenden Daten ergeben sich
keine Hinweise auf die Anwesenheit von Fentanylen oder anderen synthetischen Opioiden im untersuchten
Material." This does not contradict the Europe-wide nitazene picture below, but it does mean the
contamination warning should not be written as though it were uniform across Europe.

## EUDA — European Drug Report 2025: Heroin and other opioids / Overdose deaths, nitazenes — https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2025/heroin-and-other-opioids_en — accessed 2026-08-31

Replaces the bare `https://www.euda.europa.eu/` homepage link previously cited for the contamination item,
which did not carry the claim.

Nitazene opioids caused localised poisoning outbreaks in **Ireland** (2023, 2024) and **France** (2023),
with clusters or significant numbers of deaths and acute toxicity also reported in **Germany** (2024) and
**Sweden and Norway** (2023/24). In **Sweden**, over 30 deaths associated with metonitazene were reported
between January 2023 and September 2024. In **Estonia**, drug-induced deaths rose from 82 (2022) to 119
(2023), with nitazenes — mostly metonitazene and protonitazene — implicated in **52 %** of them.

In France and Ireland the nitazenes appear to have been **mis-sold as heroin**, causing inadvertent
consumption and multiple overdoses; in Ireland they were also mis-sold as benzodiazepines in 2024. Most
supply is believed to originate in China. Because of their potency and novelty, nitazenes "may not be
routinely detected in procedures commonly used for post-mortem toxicology", so reported death counts are
probably an underestimate.

## TripSit combination matrix (local) — `resources/tripsit-combo/combos.json` — key `opioids`

Ratings for the `opioids` row, checked against the heroin profile's combination warnings:

**Dangerous:** alcohol, benzodiazepines, cocaine, GHB/GBL, ketamine, dextromethorphan, MXE,
**pregabalin**, tramadol.
**Unsafe:** diphenhydramine.
**Caution:** amphetamines, MAOIs, mephedrone, nitrous, PCP.

The profile's "never combine with alcohol, benzodiazepines, GHB/GBL, ketamine" and its speedball warning
(opioids + cocaine) are all corroborated. **Pregabalin** was the notable omission: it is rated Dangerous
here and is named explicitly in the guide's own GHB and GBL depressant lists, but was missing from heroin's,
even though a reader is least likely to classify a nerve-pain or anxiety prescription as a depressant.
