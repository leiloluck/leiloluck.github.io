## Description

Heroin (diacetylmorphin) is a semi-synthetic opioid derived from the opium poppy plant. Street heroin typically has a purity of 15-45% and is often mixed with substances like glucose, chalk, or paracetamol.

## Effects

"Anxiety-reducing, pain-relieving and relaxing" sensations including euphoria, vivid visions, and feelings of warmth and well-being between wakefulness and sleep.

## Possible Negative Effects

Mental impairment (confusion, disorientation, memory gaps), extreme constipation, reduced sexual desire, and dangerous respiratory depression (2-4 breaths per minute).

## Mechanism of Action

Heroin's high fat solubility allows rapid brain penetration, creating an intense rush ("kick") and high addiction potential. The body converts it to morphine, then excretes it via kidneys.

## Long-term Risks

Regular multi-week use causes tolerance, requiring increased amounts. Continued use leads to psychological and physical dependence. Cessation after prolonged use triggers withdrawal symptoms (8-12 hours after last use): sweating, vomiting, diarrhea, cramps, insomnia, and anxiety.

Shared injection equipment risks transmission of hepatitis and HIV.

## Contraindications

Lung diseases (asthma), liver/kidney disease, epilepsy, pregnancy, and breastfeeding.

## Drug Combinations (Dangerous)

- Heroin + other depressants (alcohol, benzodiazepines, GHB): increased overdose and respiratory failure risk
- Heroin + cocaine: opposing effects strain the body; can cause respiratory distress and cardiac failure

## Safer Use Guidelines

- Avoid combining multiple depressants
- Use drug checking services
- Start with small amounts
- Don't share smoking tubes (hepatitis/herpes transmission risk)
- Use uncoated foil when smoking
- For injection: use sterile equipment, clean injection sites, check for visible particles
