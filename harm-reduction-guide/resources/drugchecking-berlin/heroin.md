**Date accessed:** 2026-03-31

## Substance Description

Heroin is a black market product containing the active compound diamorphin (diacetylmorphin), classified as a "downer" or depressant opioid. Average street purity in Germany (2021) was approximately 18.7% diamorphin base, with significant variation (10-72% reported). Common adulterants include caffeine and paracetamol.

## Routes of Administration

| Route | Onset | Notes |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Injection (IV) | Few seconds | Rapid effects, highest overdose risk |
| Smoking (foil) | ~30 seconds | Considerably safer than injection, minimal overdose danger |
| Snorting (nasal) | Minutes | Lower overdose risk than injection, causes nasal damage |
| Rectal | 30-60 seconds | Relatively infection-safe but overdose possible |
| Oral | Uncommon | Tablets exist for medical substitution only |

## Dosage Information

- Lethal dose for non-tolerant individuals via IV: approximately 30 mg diamorphine hydrochloride
- Opioid tolerance diminishes nearly completely after 5 days abstinence

## Primary Effects

"Glücksgefühl" (happiness), warmth, relaxation, emotional detachment, anxiety/worry reduction, described as "feeling surrounded by a protective cloak."

## Acute Side Effects

Constricted pupils, headache, dizziness, sweating, constipation, skin reactions, respiratory depression, nausea/vomiting, muscle cramps, blood pressure fluctuations, seizures (particularly with rapid injection).

## Overdose Symptoms

"Life-threatening respiratory depression with breathing slowdown," blue-tinted cold skin, blood pressure collapse, shock, unconsciousness, muscle paralysis, circulatory failure, coma, death.

## Long-term Effects

Weight loss, sexual dysfunction, rapid tolerance development, psychological/physical dependence with withdrawal symptoms, vein damage (IV use), abscesses, respiratory damage (inhalation), possible brain lesions, nasal inflammation (snorting), infectious disease risk (HIV, hepatitis C).

## Safer Use Recommendations

- Test heroin for potency and dangerous contaminants
- Never mix with other depressants (alcohol, benzodiazepines, GHB, ketamine, other opioids)
- "Avoid consumption alone"; use supervised consumption facilities when available
- Carry naloxone nasal spray; educate contacts on proper use
- Use sterile, personal needles/water/filters; never share equipment
- For snorting: use personal rounded-tip tube; minimise crystal size; rinse nasal passages regularly
- Before smoking: assess purity by melting behaviour
- For rectal use: test cautiously due to overdose possibility

## Drug Interactions

Particularly dangerous: speedball (heroin + cocaine), concurrent depressants (alcohol, benzodiazepines, GHB/GBL, ketamine, gabapentinoids like pregabalin), MAO inhibitors.

## Contraindications

Respiratory disease/depression, unknown severe abdominal pain, bowel obstruction, elevated intracranial pressure, pheochromocytoma, pregnancy/lactation, epilepsy, liver/kidney damage, untreated hypothyroidism, low blood pressure, bradycardia, prostate enlargement, diabetes.

## Detection Windows

Blood: 24 hours | Saliva: 24 hours (diamorphin) to 5 days (metabolites) | Urine: 3-7 days
